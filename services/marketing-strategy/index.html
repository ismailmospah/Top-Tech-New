Marketing strategy

Most marketing budgets are not wasted on bad execution. They are wasted on execution that was never pointed anywhere. Strategy is the part that decides who you are talking to, what you are saying, and where — before a single post or ad is made.

Start a project → All services

Overview

What marketing strategy actually means

A marketing strategy is a set of decisions, not a document. It answers four questions: who is the customer worth winning, what do we want them to believe, which channels reach them affordably, and how will we know it is working.

Everything downstream depends on those answers. Content becomes easier to plan when you know who it is for. Creative gets sharper when the message is settled. Paid media stops being a guessing game when the audience and the offer are defined. That is why we treat strategy as the first service rather than an add-on.

We work in the Saudi and Egyptian markets specifically, which matters more than it sounds. Buying behaviour, platform mix, price sensitivity and the language people actually search in differ between the two — and often between segments inside each one.

Audience

Who this is for

  • Businesses spending on marketing without a clear plan — posting regularly, running ads, and still unsure what is driving results.
  • Startups preparing to launch that need positioning and a channel plan before they commit budget.
  • Established brands entering a new market — an Egyptian company expanding into Saudi Arabia, or the reverse.
  • Companies with an internal marketing team that executes well but needs direction and an outside perspective.
  • Brands whose message has drifted — where sales, marketing and the website each describe the company differently.

Problems

The problems it solves

Activity without direction

Everyone is busy — posts go out, ads run, designs get made — but nobody can explain how any of it connects to a business goal.

Talking to everyone

When the audience is defined as 'anyone who might buy', the message has to be generic. Generic messages are ignored by everyone equally.

Channel by default

Being on a platform because competitors are, not because the audience is there or the economics work.

No way to judge results

Without agreed goals and measures, every report becomes a debate about whether the numbers are good.

Inconsistent positioning

The sales pitch, the website and the social feed each promise something different, so nothing sticks in memory.

Budget spread too thin

Small amounts across six channels usually beats nothing, but loses to a focused spend on the two that matter.

Scope

What we do

Market and competitor review

Where you sit against the alternatives a customer is genuinely comparing you to — including the option of doing nothing.

Audience definition

Real segments with real buying triggers and objections, not demographic placeholders.

Positioning and messaging

A clear statement of what you offer, to whom, and why it is worth choosing — written so the whole team can repeat it.

Channel plan

Which platforms, what role each one plays, and roughly how effort and budget should split between them.

Content direction

The themes and formats worth committing to, so content planning stops starting from a blank page every month.

Measurement framework

The handful of numbers that actually indicate progress, and what a reasonable expectation looks like for each.

Process

How we work

  1. Discovery

    We start with your side of the story: the business model, margins, what has been tried, what sells today and who your best customers already are.

  2. Research

    Market, competitors and audience — including how people search and talk about your category in Arabic and in English.

  3. Positioning workshop

    We bring options rather than one answer, and settle the positioning with you in the room. Decisions made together survive contact with reality.

  4. The plan

    Channels, messaging, content direction, budget split and measures — written as something to execute, not to admire.

  5. Handover or execution

    Your team runs it, or we do. If we do, the plan becomes the brief for social, content, creative and media buying.

  6. Review

    Strategy that is never revisited becomes decoration. We set a review point to check what the market taught us and adjust.

Deliverables

What you get

  • A written strategy document covering positioning, audience, messaging and channels
  • Audience segments with buying triggers and objections
  • A messaging framework your sales and marketing can both use
  • A channel plan with the role and rough budget weighting of each
  • Content themes and priority formats
  • A measurement framework naming the metrics that matter
  • A working session to walk the team through all of it

Questions

Common questions

How long does a marketing strategy take?

It depends on how much research the market needs and how quickly decisions can be made on your side. A focused strategy for a single market and product line moves faster than one covering several segments across Saudi Arabia and Egypt.

The variable that slows projects most is not our workload — it is access to the people who can approve positioning. We plan around that from the start.

Do we need a strategy if we already post regularly?

Posting regularly is execution. It answers how often, not why this and not something else. If you cannot explain in one sentence who your content is for and what you want them to believe, strategy is the missing piece — and it usually makes the same budget work harder rather than requiring more.

Can you build a strategy for both Saudi Arabia and Egypt at once?

Yes, and it is a common request. We treat them as related but separate: shared positioning where it genuinely holds, and separate audience, channel and messaging decisions where the markets differ. Pretending one plan fits both is how brands end up sounding foreign in both.

What happens after the strategy is delivered?

Either your team executes it, or we do. When we execute, the strategy becomes the standing brief for social media marketing, content, design and media buying — which is the main reason clients keep both under one roof.

Do you work with startups?

Yes. For early-stage companies we keep the strategy deliberately narrow — one audience, one message, one or two channels — because the real goal at that stage is learning quickly, not covering every base.
